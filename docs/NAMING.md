# The name

Checked 2026-08-08, because "geistshell" sounds like "Ghost in the Shell" and
the question will come up again. It is written down so the next naming decision
in the `geist*` family does not re-litigate it.

**This is orientation, not legal advice. No lawyer was involved and the
registers were not queried successfully — see the open item at the bottom.**

## Ghost in the Shell is not the risk

Confusion is judged on sound, appearance and meaning, weighted by how close the
goods are.

- **Sound** — "geist-shell", two syllables, against "ghost-in-the-shell", four
  words. Different opening, different rhythm.
- **Appearance** — one word against four.
- **Meaning** — the only bridge: a German speaker reads Geist as ghost. But the
  distinctive part of the title is the "in the", and conceptual proximity alone
  rarely carries confusion when sound and appearance diverge.
- **"shell" is descriptive here.** Bourne shell, PowerShell, fish, nushell.
  Descriptive elements count for little in a similarity comparison.

Title protection (§ 5 MarkenG) covers confusion *about the work*. A C23 agent
runtime is not a manga, an anime or a game. The distance in goods is large.

It would only get uncomfortable if this project moved toward entertainment or
consumer software — there are games under that brand, so class 9 is occupied
there. It does not.

## The risk, if there is one, is "Geist"

`geist` is the house element: geistlib, geistagent, geistwissen, geistshell,
geisten. A conflict there hits the whole family at once, which is why it — and
not the "shell" half — is the thing worth checking.

One candidate seen while looking: **Vertiv sells data-centre PDUs and their
monitoring software under the brand "Geist"** (geistglobal.com). That is IT
hardware with monitoring software, so class 9 is plausibly occupied and the
goods sit far closer to ours than anything in the Ghost in the Shell franchise.

Nice class 9 (software as a good) and class 42 (software development as a
service) are assessed separately, so an application of our own would likely need
both.

## Standing position

- **Open source: use the name.** A non-commercial repository, in a field where
  "shell" is a generic term, is not a realistic target. If it ever had to
  change, the cost is a `git mv` and a redirect.
- **Before commercialisation:** the moment we want `geisten` registered as a
  mark is the same moment somebody runs a similarity search across the whole
  family. That is a patent attorney's job, it costs a low three-figure sum, and
  it covers all five names in one pass. Doing it piecemeal beforehand is unpaid
  work with residual uncertainty.

## Open

The registers were **not** checked. Both are free:

- DPMAregister (dpma.de) for DE
- TMview (tmdn.org) for EU, national and international registers in one query

Search `geist*` as a word component, restricted to Nice classes 9 and 42. Do
not search `geistshell` — it will find nothing and prove nothing. The hit that
would matter is plainly "GEIST".
